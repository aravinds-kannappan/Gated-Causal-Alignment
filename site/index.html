Consult a neighbor only when its evidence would change the action

A robot arm, a mobile base and a verifier each see a different part of a drawer task's causal graph. A value-of-alignment gate opens an edge to a neighbor only when the expected decision gain beats the declared cost. This page replays real simulator episodes and reports every result from the runs.

The idea in one formula

Three layers of one control loop. Observation refinement decides what one agent should look at. Fusion decides how agents combine beliefs. The gate sits between them and decides whom to align with, per decision.

VOAij(t) = E[ U(a*(bi ⊕ bj)) ] − E[ U(a*(bi)) ] − cij

The arm holds beliefs p (the cabinet will slide) and q (the next pull crosses the force limit). A neighbor's report is a binary signal with calibrated true-positive and true-negative rates. The gate computes, before asking, the expected utility of acting on the fused belief minus the utility of acting now, minus the declared cost. It opens the edge with the largest positive value, updates, and repeats until no edge clears its cost.

This is the single-agent refinement criterion with "a finer observation" replaced by "my belief plus a neighbor's report". The same precondition carries over: an edge has strict value only when the arm's belief aliases cases with different optimal actions and the neighbor's report separates them. An exhaustive finite check over 4,332 belief-accuracy cases agreed with that precondition in 4,326; the 6 remaining cases are exact ties between two equally good actions.

Two experiments

Stage A isolates the gate's logic with exact enumeration over a synthetic causal model. Stage B runs it inside a physics drawer task with learned specialist models and held-out configurations.

Stage A: synthetic POSCM, exact

Force drives handle displacement; the drawer or the cabinet moves; a contact state and a force limit are exogenous. The arm sees force and handle motion, the base sees cabinet displacement, the verifier sees force against the limit. Worlds, the arm's own evidence and every neighbor report are enumerated with their probabilities, so each number is an expectation, not a sample. Sweeps: the arm's own evidence quality (subgraph overlap), the edge cost, the prior, and the number of specialists up to 12 with redundant and irrelevant neighbors.

Stage B: physics drawer task

A MuJoCo scene: a cabinet on a slide joint with declared ground friction (anchored or free), a drawer with rail resistance, a heavy base whose plate braces the cabinet, and a pull modelled as an external force pair. Skills: pull (ramp force until the handle moves, then hold), reposition (brace), release (back off below the known or prior limit). Specialists fit class-balanced logistic models on 35,409 training steps from 72 training configurations; report rates are calibrated there. Evaluation: 24 held-out configurations (three unseen resistances, anchored and free cabinets, two sensor-noise levels, two handle variants) × 3 seeds = 72 episodes per controller.

Interpretation

The gate keeps the decision and drops the traffic. On held-out configurations the gate matched always-align on compliant completion (67%), utility (0.886 vs 0.888) and stops, preserved 99.4% of its decisions, and sent 27% fewer messages at three specialists. The oracle reference shows more headroom (0.57 messages per decision): the gate is conservative because the arm's own evidence is weak, so the base edge is usually worth its cost.
Cost scales with decision-relevant edges, not with agents. From three to five specialists the gate's messages stayed at 1.06 per decision while always-align rose to 2.46; in Stage A the gate held near 1.7 edges per decision up to twelve specialists. The redundant head camera and the irrelevant torso were never consulted.
The fusion rule matters more than the gate. Replacing exact fusion with the fixed-weight CAN diffusion pool cut utility by 43% and decision preservation to 68% even with every edge open, cost 88 messages per decision, and collapsed at five specialists. This reproduces, inside a robot task, the earlier Pursuit finding that pooled evidence beats fixed-weight mixing, and it says the gate should sit in front of an exact or confidence-weighted fusion rule.
Isolation costs completion and time, not violations. Every controller, including the isolated arm, recorded zero violations on held-out configurations: the arm's own learned model is cautious enough to brace early. Isolation instead showed up as stalls (33% completion) and unnecessary stops (0.48 per decision). The verifier edge's value in this task is the limit it carries: an arm that releases without it backs off to the prior and never recovers.
A centralized network is as good here, on quality. One classifier over all features matched always-align on every quality metric. The modular gate's advantage is on cost and on robustness to a corrupted neighbor, not on decision quality. That is a fair result to report and it narrows the claim.
Limits. Report accuracies are state-independent calibrations; the task has two binary latents; the horizon grid shows no family dependence because both cabinet types were braced early; the process-violation advantage did not materialise because no controller violated. These bound what the numbers support.

Next steps and research direction

State-dependent report accuracies. Replace the calibrated constants with accuracies conditioned on the neighbor's own evidence strength, so the gate can see when a neighbor currently knows nothing (a base before any force is applied). This should close part of the gap to the oracle reference.
Confidence-weighted fusion behind the gate. Run the gate in front of a confidence-weighted CAN update and a convergence-matched diffusion, so the fusion axis is a fair contest rather than a fixed-weight pool.
Learned subgraphs. Discover each specialist's subgraph online instead of declaring it, and let the gate consume the discovered overlap structure. The gate's cost claim predicts that cost should track the discovered number of decision-relevant edges.
Harder process constraints. Add constraints that the arm's own model cannot anticipate (a sudden change in cabinet anchoring mid-episode, a limit that changes with contact state), so the safety value of alignment is measured, not only its efficiency.
Shared harness. Package the Task (environment, subgraph assignment, verifier spec, cost model) and Solver (gate, fusion) interfaces so the same gate runs on the grid-pursuit and real-time-strategy environments the other workstreams use, and on a full mobile-manipulation simulator.
Sequential gate. The gate is one-step. A sequential version values an edge by its effect on later beliefs and probes, which the formal framework already defines.

Related work

Verified sources and what this work takes from each.

  • Networks of Causal Abstractions: A Sheaf-theoretic Framework (Gabriele D'Acunto, Paolo Di Lorenzo, Sergio Barbarossa; arXiv 2509.25236)
    Source of the causal abstraction network and sheaf-consistency diffusion that our gate decides whether to run against each neighbor.
  • Toward a spectral theory of cellular sheaves (Jakob Hansen, Robert Ghrist; Journal of Applied and Computational Topology 3, 315-358)
    Provides the sheaf Laplacian and harmonic-section theory underlying consensus of local causal models across the agent graph.
  • Abstracting Causal Models (Sander Beckers, Joseph Y. Halpern; AAAI)
    Defines constructive causal abstraction; our edge maps between specialists' models are abstractions in this sense.
  • Causal Policy Gradient for Whole-Body Mobile Manipulation (Jiaheng Hu, Peter Stone, Roberto Martin-Martin; RSS)
    Causal MoMa discovers action-to-reward causal dependencies for whole-body control; our specialists carry such local causal models.
  • A non-divergent estimation algorithm in the presence of unknown correlations (Simon J. Julier, Jeffrey K. Uhlmann; American Control Conference)
    Covariance intersection fuses Gaussian beliefs with unknown shared information, avoiding double-counting a common prior.
  • Learning when to Communicate at Scale in Multiagent Cooperative and Competitive Tasks (Amanpreet Singh, Tushar Jain, Sainbayar Sukhbaatar; ICLR)
    IC3Net learns a binary communication gate per agent; we contrast its learned gate with an explicit VOI criterion.
  • Learning Attentional Communication for Multi-Agent Cooperation (Jiechuan Jiang, Zongqing Lu; NeurIPS)
    ATOC's attention unit decides when to form communication groups; a learned precursor to our acquisition gate.
  • Learning Individually Inferred Communication for Multi-Agent Cooperation (Ziluo Ding, Tiejun Huang, Zongqing Lu; NeurIPS)
    I2C infers one-to-one communication need via causal effect of a neighbor on one's policy, closest in spirit to our gate.
  • Context-Aware Sparse Deep Coordination Graphs (Tonghan Wang, Liang Zeng, Weijun Dong, Qianlan Yang, Yang Yu, Chongjie Zhang; ICLR)
    Sparsifies coordination graphs by a variance-of-payoff criterion; we sparsify alignment edges by VOI instead.
  • Defining and Characterizing Reward Gaming (Joar Skalse, Nikolaus H. R. Howe, Dmitrii Krasheninnikov, David Krueger; NeurIPS)
    Formalizes proxy-reward hacking; motivates our process verifier as a guard against optimizing a hackable alignment proxy.
  • Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding (Mohammed Alshiekh, Roderick Bloem, Rudiger Ehlers, Bettina Konighofer, Scott Niekum, Ufuk Topcu; AAAI)
    Shield that overrides unsafe actions against a temporal-logic spec; our force-limit and cabinet-motion verifier plays this role.
  • Information Value Theory (Ronald A. Howard; IEEE Transactions on Systems Science and Cybernetics 2(1), 22-26)
    Origin of expected value of information; our gate thresholds the VOI of a neighbor's causal evidence against alignment cost.
  • Principles of Metareasoning (Stuart J. Russell, Eric Wefald; Artificial Intelligence 49(1-3), 361-395)
    Rational metareasoning: treat alignment computations as actions chosen by expected utility gain minus time cost.
  • Reasoning About Beliefs and Actions Under Computational Resource Constraints (Eric J. Horvitz; UAI)
    Decision-theoretic control of inference under resource limits, which motivates gating rather than always fusing neighbors.
  • Act-Then-Measure: Reinforcement Learning for Partially Observable Environments with Active Measuring (Merlijn Krale, Thiago D. Simao, Nils Jansen; ICAPS)
    Modern RL formulation where measuring is a costed action bounded by value of information; we gate evidence acquisition the same way.
